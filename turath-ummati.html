Wagag

Episode 1 · 20 photos

The Forgotten Village That Gave Birth to an Empire

What can a teacher and seven students do? Unite Morocco?

It sounds like the opening of a legend. It is actually the true beginning of one of the greatest chapters in the history of the Maghreb.

Summer has arrived, and most people are heading to the coast to cool off by the sea. In the first episode of the Turath Ummah series, Zakaria invites you to turn your back on the beach at Aglou, near Tiznit, and climb into the hills of Sous. There, between valleys, stands a place that is not widely known but should never be overlooked.

Nearly a thousand years ago, a Sufi, scholar and teacher named Wagag Ibn Zallu al Lamti founded a zawiya here. He did not build it for fame. He built it to teach, to guide and to educate. Students came from across Sous and beyond to sit at his feet. One of them left with a vision that would change the course of history, and a state would rise whose reach stretched from the Senegal River all the way to Andalusia.

But this episode is about more than one empire. It takes you inside the traditional schools, a world with no desks, no blackboards and no chalk, only circles of students on the floor, each group exploring a different field. Some call it the first university the world ever knew. So why did colonial powers decide to shut it down?

And the question that makes the story urgent: does this zawiya still live today, or has the voice of history gone silent?

Our heritage is not only written in books. It is alive in places many of us have forgotten.

Press play and walk through the door.

Saadian Sugar

Episode 2 · 44 photos

The Sweet Secret That Built an Empire

Imagine Morocco as one of the biggest sugar exporters in the world. Great empires were built on it, and palaces rose from it. Some of them still stand today. But how did it happen?

Five hundred years ago, Morocco faced an enemy it had never seen before. Foreign fleets were seizing the coast, one city after another, while the country was divided and weak. In the south, tribes that had rarely stood together made a decision that would change everything, and from it a new dynasty was born, the Saadians. Their story is unusual, because power came looking for them rather than the other way around.

But power needs an army, and an army needs money. So where does a young state find a fortune? In this episode, Zakaria follows the answer to the countryside near Essaouira, a place that is not far from the crowds, yet almost no one knows it. Hidden there is the trace of a once mighty industry that reached the farthest lands on earth.

Walk through the ruins of giant factories. Follow canals that carried water across dozens of kilometers. Discover how raw cane became “sugar as pure as milk” that European ships crossed the sea to buy. And hear about the true price of that sweetness, which was never measured in gold alone.

Along the way, Zakaria stands where history was left to rest, and finds out just how alone these treasures really are.

This is not only a story about money and power. It is a tribute to the people who made the sugar, and to what they left behind.

Press play and taste the history.

The Saadians

Episode 3 · 2 photos

The Fortress That Fell: The Battle for Santa Cruz

Imagine a mighty Portuguese fortress, armed with heavy cannons and the most modern weapons of its age. Around it stand ordinary men from Sous, carrying swords and spears. They have one goal, which is to take back their land. They do not know fear, and they do not know the meaning of surrender.

You may have stood on the Kasbah of Agadir Oufella and admired the view of the bay. Most visitors see ruins and the scars of the 1960 earthquake. In this episode, Zakaria sees something more: a battlefield that shaped the history of Morocco.

The story begins in 1505, when a Portuguese explorer arrives, falls in love with a spring of fresh water and builds a fort around it. His name for the spot still lives on in a famous Agadir neighborhood today. From there, the coast is swallowed one fortress at a time, and the people of Sous are left to face cannons with almost nothing.

Then the tide begins to turn. There are daring raids, sudden betrayals and a night when cunning proves deadlier than any cannon. There is a truce that sparks one big question: was it a surrender, or the calm before a storm? And then comes a siege on a scale the region had rarely seen, with a vast army gathered on the hills above the fortress.

How did a people with so little bring down a fort with so much? And what did the Portuguese themselves write about their enemies, compared with what the Moroccans wrote about theirs?

Some victories are won with weapons. Some are won with unity and faith.

Press play and climb the Kasbah with us.

Iguiliz

Episode 4 · Teaser

The Forgotten Summit That Shook an Empire

Can an idea born in the mountains bring down an entire empire?

Imagine a power that stretched from the plains of Senegal to the palaces of Andalusia, gathering cities, horsemen and fortunes under one banner. It ruled half of the Islamic West. Who could possibly topple a giant like that?

The answer begins on a rocky peak in the High Atlas called Iguiliz. There are no palaces here and no armies. Just wind, stone and silence, and the ruins of a place nearly nine hundred years old. Yet from this forgotten summit, a spark was lit that would change the course of Moroccan history.

In this episode, Zakaria climbs the mountain and follows the footsteps of a man most Moroccans have heard of, but few truly know. We trace his journey from a village in the Atlas across the known world, through some of the great centers of learning of his age, and back home again, burning with knowledge and conviction. We meet his famously fiery character, a loyal student at his side, and a heated clash in the heart of Marrakech that left the authorities with a very difficult decision.

Then we step inside the ruins themselves. Look for the place of retreat, the prayer hall with its strict order, and the open square where a leader once addressed his followers. And then comes the day he made the boldest announcement of his entire life.

What did he say? Why did it change everything? And what did he leave behind, in stone, in ideas and in the minds of generations?

It is a story of reform and bloodshed, of ruin and brilliance, and of one question that still echoes through the Atlas: does history change through ideas, or through the people who carry them?

Press play and climb to the summit with us.
